## Executive Summary

Public sentiment around SpaceX (SPCX) over the past week is sharply **polarized and volatile**. The company's first earnings call as a public company triggered a significant negative repricing (-12% to -14% in a single session), driven less by weak fundamentals than by investor anxiety over massive AI capital spending and Musk's declaration that SpaceX will use Nvidia chips exclusively for orbital data centers. Simultaneously, retail investors and high-profile funds (notably Cathie Wood's ARK Invest) are **buying the dip**, creating a classic "smart money worried, retail accumulating" dynamic. Net sentiment skews **cautiously bearish near-term, cautiously bullish long-term**, with elevated event risk.

## Narrative Arc Over the Week

### Phase 1: Pre-Earnings Anticipation (Late July – Early Aug)
Leading into SpaceX's inaugural public earnings, coverage was dominated by the AI narrative. Musk and management had previously floated trillion-dollar AI revenue projections, positioning SpaceX not merely as a launch provider but as an orbital AI infrastructure play. Broader market sentiment toward AI momentum stocks was already fraying — technology shares linked to the AI boom faced profit-taking as earnings season progressed. SpaceX entered its report with high expectations and a stretched narrative.

### Phase 2: Earnings Shock and Narrative Reversal (Aug 5–6)
SpaceX reported what multiple outlets described as a "very solid inaugural quarter." Quarterly AI capital spending climbed to **$15.8 billion**, with management claiming faster-than-expected returns on AI investments. Despite positive operating metrics, the market reaction was decisively negative:

- Stock fell **12–14%** in the session following the call.
- Elon Musk lost an estimated **$87 billion** in personal net worth in a single day.
- Shares shed prior-session gains and continued declining into the next trading day.

The catalyst was not earnings miss but **narrative whiplash**: Musk's statement that SpaceX will build data centers **exclusively** using Nvidia graphics chips. Coverage framed this as a double-edged sword — validating Nvidia's dominance while signaling SpaceX's enormous, ongoing cash burn on AI infrastructure that Starlink must subsidize. Reuters characterized investor worry: "how long its Starlink business can continue to bankroll costly investments in data centers and Nvidia chips."

Headlines captured the mood bluntly: *"This One Word On SpaceX's First Earnings Call Cost It 11%, And Sent Nvidia Higher"* and *"SpaceX stock declines post-earnings. Are investors worried about AI spending?"*

### Phase 3: Lockup Expiration and Structural Overhang (Aug 6)
Sentiment on Aug 6 shifted from earnings disappointment to **supply-side fear**. Up to **911.5 million insider shares** unlocked, more than doubling the freely tradable float. A separate tranche remains frozen because the stock trades below the $135 IPO price. Barron's, WSJ, and Quartz all warned investors to "brace for more volatility." The stock was already down roughly **50% from highs** and **20% from IPO** before the unlock.
