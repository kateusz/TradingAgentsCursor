## 2. What people are talking about

### 2.1 Dominant narrative: earnings quality and franchise cash

The loudest local headlines frame Q2 as a **surprise to the upside**, not a routine print:

- Net profit of **PLN 142.9 million** versus consensus **PLN 99.3 million** — **about 44% above forecasts** (*Mocne wyniki CD Projektu…*, Bankier).
- Follow-on coverage is explicitly emotional in tone: *Analitycy oczarowani wynikami* (“analysts enchanted / dazzled by results”), with the kicker that **franchises are starting to pay well**.

That language matters for sentiment. “Enchanted” is not a valuation word; it is a social-proof word. It tells retail and momentum readers that the professional crowd is impressed. Combined with a 44% beat, the likely public takeaway is: *the Witcher/Cyberpunk IP engine is no longer a hope — it is showing up in the P&L.*

### 2.2 Second narrative: secret projects already earning money

CFO-level commentary (Piotr Nielubowicz) that **part of H1 2026 IP-licensing revenue relates to unannounced projects** is the week’s most tradeable gossip item.

Why it moves sentiment:

- It implies **pipeline optionality that is not yet in the public product map**.
- It invites speculation (new Witcher, new Cyberpunk, or adjacent licenses) without giving a name — classic social-media fuel even when the only published source is Bankier.
- It reframes licensing from “back-catalog merchandising” to “forward-looking franchise development.”

The companion piece *Co szykuje CD Projekt? Setki milionów złotych na nowe gry i tajne projekty* (“hundreds of millions of zlotys for new games and secret projects”) reinforces a **spend-and-build** identity: management is capitalizing the studio, not harvesting a one-off beat.

### 2.3 Third narrative: product demand for *Witcher 3: Songs of the Past*

Management presented **900,000 wishlists across all platforms** for *Wiedźmin 3: Pieśni przeszłości*. That is a concrete, fan-visible metric.

Sentiment implication:

- Community interest in a **Witcher 3 expansion/content drop** is measurable and large.
- It supports the “attach rate” confidence mentioned in the same Bankier cluster (management believes in a strong attach rate for *Songs of the Past*).
- For traders, wishlists are a **leading demand proxy**, not a revenue print. The public conversation will treat 900k as proof of heat; the risk is that wishlists overstate conversion.

### 2.4 Corporate-admin layer (low social heat)

PAP MediaRoom hits in the same search window include:

- Registration of a **statute change and company-name change** (`cd projekt sa 122026`).
- Board **recommendation on net profit distribution** (`cd projekt sa 62026`).
- Older AGM / scale-and-structure filings (2023–2024) that look like **index/search bleed**, not this week’s news.

The profit-distribution recommendation is the only admin item with potential retail interest (dividend vs. reinvestment). It is **not** driving headline tone this week; earnings and secret IP are.

## 5. Implications for traders and investors

### 5.1 Near-term tape (days to a few weeks)

1. **Narrative tailwind is intact.** As long as no contradictory filing or gameplay leak appears, the default public story is “beat + IP flywheel + hidden pipeline.” That supports dip-buying psychology on CDR.WA rather than “sell the news,” unless the stock already fully priced the beat before this coverage.
2. **Speculative premium.** Nielubowicz’s unannounced-project comment is a **sentiment multiplier**. It can extend the rally beyond a one-day earnings pop because it cannot be immediately disproved. It can also reverse hard if a later disclosure is underwhelming (small license, not a new AAA).
3. **Watchlist as a crowd thermometer.** 900k wishlists will be repeated in forums as a bullish fact. Traders should treat it as **engagement, not bookings**. A later attach-rate miss would flip this week’s positive social proof into a disappointment narrative.

### 5.2 Positioning read-through

- **Momentum / event-driven:** Bias is long-sentiment. Crowded optimism after a 44% profit beat plus “secret IP” language raises **gap-risk on any disappointment** (guidance, delay, or quiet update).
- **Fundamental holders:** Tone supports the multi-year franchise-compounding thesis (Witcher catalog + new content + licensing). The “hundreds of millions for new games” line is **reinvestment-positive** for growth investors and **dividend-capping** for income investors pending the profit-distribution recommendation details.
- **Skeptical / short-term mean reversion:** The absence of negative headlines is not evidence that risks vanished. It is evidence that **this week’s news flow did not air them**. Fade only if price has already discounted a perfect franchise outcome.

### 5.3 What would change the mood

Sentiment is one-sided; the next narrative shift is likely to come from **new information**, not from this week’s articles aging:

- Named reveal (or leak) of the unannounced licensed projects — confirmation vs. “it was just a small deal.”
- *Songs of the Past* attach-rate, reviews, or launch-week Steam/console chatter.
- Any slippage language on the two projects / “secret” slate.
- Clarity on profit distribution (payout vs. retained earnings for the new-game spend).

## 8. Financial history — the cycle the numbers describe

This is the same pattern CD Projekt has shown between *Witcher 3* and *Cyberpunk*, and after *Cyberpunk*: **reported profits stay high longer than FCF does**, then earnings catch down when the catalog ages and amortization of the new title begins (or if a delay triggers impairment).
