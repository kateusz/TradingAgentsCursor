## Countering the Bear Case: Point by Point

### Bear argument #1: "Valuation is too high — 31.6x trailing P/E with beta 2.22"

**Bull response:** Trailing P/E is the wrong lens. Forward P/E is **16.4x** with PEG **0.57** — among the cheapest growth-adjusted large caps in the market. NVIDIA is not priced for today's earnings; it is priced for **near-doubling of EPS** to $12.89. If they deliver — and they just grew EPS 214% YoY — the stock re-rates higher, not lower. Beta 2.22 cuts both ways: in an AI bull market, high beta is a feature. Size positions accordingly, but do not confuse volatility with broken fundamentals.

### Bear argument #2: "Insider selling — Huang, Stevens, Kress all selling"

**Bull response:** Zero open-market insider **buying** is common for mega-cap tech with heavy equity compensation. Huang's sales are **10b5-1 pre-planned**; his March 2026 gift of 58.9M shares was charitable, not a market exit. Director Mark Stevens sold ~$407M in June near $209–222 — notable, but against a company buying back **$19.3B/quarter**. Net insider flow is dwarfed by corporate repurchases reducing share count. Insiders are diversifying generational wealth; the company is concentrating ownership via buybacks. Factor into position sizing, not thesis destruction.

### Bear argument #3: "Inventory and receivables are ballooning — $25.8B inventory, $40.7B A/R"

**Bull response:** Inventory grew 128% YoY and A/R grew 84% YoY — but revenue grew **85% YoY**. Yes, monitor inventory/revenue ratio (~32% vs. ~26% a year ago). But in a supply-constrained market where AWS says supply is spoken for through 2028, inventory is strategic positioning, not obsolescence risk. ### Bear argument #4: "Customer concentration — hyperscaler capex cycles will pause"

### Bear argument #5: "Competition — AMD, custom ASICs, Qualcomm edge chips"

**Bull response:** AMD beat and got called "not exceptional." Qualcomm/Multiverse edge optimization is a long-term watch item, not a near-term revenue threat. Custom ASICs serve specific hyperscaler workloads but have not broken CUDA's ecosystem moat or NVIDIA's 75% gross margins. Every SMCI rack still anchors on NVIDIA GPUs. SpaceX chose exclusivity, not a multi-vendor hedge. Competition is real but **not yet threatening pricing power or share at the high end** — the only segment that matters for NVDA's earnings power.

### Bear argument #6: "Michael Burry is short and warns of 1987 crash"

**Bull response:** Burry's short is **underwater**. His macro crash thesis is systemic, not NVDA-specific. In a 1987-style deleveraging, NVDA's index weight and beta make it a liquidation candidate regardless of fundamentals — that is a tail risk to hedge, not a reason to short a company printing $48.6B quarterly FCF. The contrarian read: famous shorts are **not being validated** on NVDA. Sentiment data explicitly notes Burry is "increasingly isolated" on this name while the AI trade "roars back to life."

### Bear argument #7: "Energy and supply-chain bottlenecks will cap deployment"

**Bull response:** Huang's "1,000x more energy" comment frames AI as a **multi-decade industrial transformation** — bullish for TAM, not bearish for demand.
