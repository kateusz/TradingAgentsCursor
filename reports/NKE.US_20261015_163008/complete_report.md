## How to trade it

Primary trend is **down**. Tactical momentum is **up**, and the tactical long is already extended.

**Do not chase the 36.94–37.07 print.** Price is on the upper band, RSI has jumped to 57 in one session, the 50 SMA is still overhead, and the rally volume is lighter than the selloff volume. The reward to 37.50 is about 0.55 against a 2 ATR stop near 34.70, which is a poor payoff if the entry is here.

**Countertrend long, only on a reset.** Prefer a pullback that holds **35.30–35.50** (10 EMA and Bollinger middle) with RSI staying above roughly 45 and the histogram staying positive. Entry zone 35.40, invalidation on a close under **34.60** (VWMA / 2026-10-14 low), which is about 1.5–2.0 ATR of risk from that entry. First target is a retest of **37.00–37.50**. Only raise the target toward **38.50–40.00** after a daily close above the 50 SMA on volume at or above the recent median (~19M shares). If 35.30 fails on the first retest, stand aside; the 10 EMA turn is then a one-week event.

**Fade / defensive short, only at resistance.** A rejection from **37.00–37.50** (upper band and 50 SMA) while the histogram stops expanding is a short back toward **35.40**, then **34.60**. Invalidation is a daily close above the 50 SMA, with a working stop near **37.80–38.00** so a normal ATR poke through 37.49 does not knock the trade out. Do not press a short if MACD is accelerating toward zero and volume expands on the push through 37.50 — that is the setup migrating from "bounce" to "first higher low."

**Flat is a position.** Between 35.50 and 37.00 the market is in the middle of a compressed post-crash range, momentum is better, and location is worse. Waiting for either the 35.40 hold or the 37.50 break is consistent with every selected indicator. Nothing in the 200-day or 50-day averages argues for a full-size investment long.

## Actionable view

**Base case is “cheap versus Nike’s history, fairly priced versus the cash and the forward EPS.”** An 18× trailing multiple and ~12× EV/EBITDA on 6.7% net margins, flat-to-down revenue, and a dividend that FY2026 free cash flow did not fully cover is a coherent price near $37. Upside needs evidence that Q4’s 49% gross margin is a new run-rate. Downside toward the **$32** low is what you get if revenue keeps slipping and the margin snaps back toward 40%, because $1.66 of forward EPS at a high-teens multiple lands near that low.

**What would make the long case concrete**

- The next reported quarter (not in this bundle) holds gross margin well above the 40–42% band that defined Q4 FY25 through Q3 FY26. Even a mid-40s print, with revenue no worse than flat, would say Q4 was a step-change rather than a one-quarter credit.
- Operating cash flow stays near or above net income without another large payable build. Receivables stabilizing after the $1.2B year-over-year increase would confirm the Q4 cash number.
- The dividend is maintained. At ~$2.4B it is the main reason the equity still has a floor. Buybacks staying at zero is acceptable while coverage is this tight.
- The $2.0B current maturity is refinanced out of cash or rolled without a change in the net-debt picture. Cash of $7.6B makes this a low-probability solvency issue.

**What would make the short or avoid case concrete**

- Gross margin back at ~40% and revenue still declining. Forward EPS of $1.66 would then be the number to underwrite, and 22× forward is not cheap for that path.
- Another quarter of receivables growth and sub-$500M operating cash flow. That would put the dividend, not the multiple, at the center of the risk.
- A dividend cut. The yield is doing more work than the growth story. Removing it while the stock is under the 200-day average would take away the holder base that replaced the old multiple-expansion buyer.

**Positioning around the levels in the snapshot.** Price is already pinned between the 52-week low ($31.97) and the 50-day average ($37.75), with the 200-day ($47.64) as the first level that would mean the derating is reversing. Fundamentally, that 200-day is not “fair value”; fair value moves with whether FY2027 earnings track $2.08 or $1.66. Until a post-May quarter is in hand, the disciplined use of this file is: underwrite the **year** (revenue ~$46B, operating margin ~8%, FCF ~$2.2B, dividend ~$2.4B), and treat Q4’s 49% gross margin and $0.72 EPS as a hypothesis, not a run-rate.

# Bull Researcher

## Round 1

Nike at $36.94 is a franchise the market has already sentenced. ### The profit turn is in the statements, not in a story

Q4 FY26 (ended 2026-05-31) versus Q4 FY25 is the evidence. Revenue was essentially flat, down 1.1% to $10.97 billion. Gross margin went from 40.3% to 49.2%, an 890 basis-point jump, because cost of revenue fell from $6.63 billion to $5.58 billion while sales barely moved. Operating income went from $321 million to $1.31 billion with operating expense almost unchanged ($4.08 billion versus $4.15 billion). Demand-creation spend did not get slashed. This was a gross-margin recovery, which is the kind of recovery a brand with pricing power and a finished destock actually produces.

The full year still looks ordinary, and that is the point. FY2026 summed to $46.4 billion of revenue, an 8.2% operating margin, $3.11 billion of net income, and $2.09 of diluted EPS. Gross margin sat in a 40–42% band for four straight quarters and then printed 49%. The snapshot prices the fade: forward EPS is $1.66, 20% below trailing EPS of $2.08, so the forward P/E of 22.3× is higher than the trailing 17.8×. Consensus, as embedded in that snapshot, does not believe Q4. A sustained mid-40s gross margin — not even a repeat of 49% — would make $1.66 the low case rather than the base case. That is the asymmetry. You do not need a revenue boom. Opex is stuck near $4.0 billion a quarter. If gross profit stays elevated on a ~$46 billion sales base, operating leverage does the growth work.

Q1 FY2027 is not in this bundle, so the 49% print is a hypothesis until the next quarter. The bull does not need to pretend otherwise. The market is not paying for the hypothesis either.
