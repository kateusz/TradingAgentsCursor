## Trend analysis

### Long-term (200 SMA)

The 200-day SMA is **246.59**, almost unchanged over the last month (246.92 on 21 Aug → 246.59 now). It is a **flat, slightly declining long-term mean**, not a rising bull market benchmark. Price has been **below it since the 25 Aug gap** and has not tested it on the way back. Distance of **~9.3 PLN (−3.8%)** is modest in ATR terms (about **1.2 ATR**), so a squeeze into the 200-day is plausible if the 50-day is reclaimed — it is not a distant moonshot.

There is **no golden cross**. The 50-day (242.37) remains **~4.2 PLN below** the 200-day. The 50-day is **creeping higher** (240.06 on 25 Aug → 242.37), while the 200-day is **edging lower**, so the gap is slowly narrowing. That is **repair**, not a cross. Until the 50-day overtakes the 200-day with price above both, swing longs are counter-trend.

### Medium-term (50 SMA)

The 50-day at **242.37** is the **first meaningful ceiling**. It has risen steadily through the post-crash window because the August 250–266 cluster is still inside the 50-day window. Price has **failed this zone twice already** in September:

- 3 Sep: opened **240.0**, high **242.0**, closed **232.7** — a full rejection.  
- 8 Sep: high **241.7**, close **239.4**, then sold off to 230.6 by 10 Sep.

A close back **above 242.4–243** would be the first medium-term evidence that the 25 Aug event is being repaired. Until then, the 50-day is **supply**, not support.

### Short-term (10 EMA)

The 10 EMA collapsed from **257.32 (21 Aug)** to a low near **235.73 (11 Sep)** and has **turned up** to **236.01**. Price closed **above it** on 11 Sep (235.4 vs 235.73 was still a touch below) and more cleanly on **14 Sep (237.3 vs 236.01)**. That is the **first tactical long trigger** in this set: short-term momentum has stopped making new lows.

Caveat: the 10 EMA is noisy after a shock. The 8 Sep bounce also lifted price toward the then-falling 10 EMA (237.99) and **failed within two sessions**. A **hold above the 10 EMA for several closes**, not a single print, is the filter.

## 3. What people are talking about (narrative map)

Five company stories dominate the week. Together they form one coherent public story: *results beat, IP is monetizing, the next Witcher product is already in demand, and management is spending into unannounced work.*

### 3.1 Earnings surprise — the loudest, most bullish tape

**Headline:** net profit in Q2 of **PLN 142.9 million vs consensus PLN 99.3 million** — **about 44% above forecasts**.

**Inferred crowd reaction:** relief plus FOMO. After years of “wait for the next big launch,” a clean beat with franchise-payoff language supports a “the wait is paying” mood rather than a “dead money until Witcher 4” mood.

### 3.2 Franchise / IP licensing — secret projects in the P&L

Management/presentation color: **~900,000 wishlist adds across all platforms** for *Wiedźmin 3: Pieśni przeszłości* (*The Witcher 3: Songs of the Past*).

**Bulls hear:** pipeline depth, conviction, franchise flywheel.  
**Bears hear:** cash going into black boxes; execution risk; “hundreds of millions” is a reminder that CDPR still bets large on unproven titles.

This week the press tilt is toward the bull reading (“what is CD Projekt preparing?” as intrigue, not as a warning).

### 3.5 Corporate housekeeping (weaker sentiment signal)

PAP items on **statute/company-name registration** and a **board recommendation on net profit distribution** are governance/dividend process, not viral. Dividend-recommendation language can support a mild “shareholder-friendly” tone among income-oriented GPW holders, but it is not the week’s emotional driver.
