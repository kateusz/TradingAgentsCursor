# Bull Researcher

## Round 1

Look, I know the chart looks ugly right now — down 13% in two weeks, below every moving average. But that's exactly where you want to be looking at a company growing revenue 65% year-over-year with a forward P/E under 7x.

Genius Sports isn't some speculative meme stock. It's the **infrastructure layer for global sports betting and broadcast data**. The Liga MX deal with Televisa this week proves they're still winning marquee partnerships in the world's most passionate soccer market. That's recurring, high-margin media-tech revenue — not a one-time license fee.

The Q2 numbers tell the real story: $195.5M revenue, normalized EBITDA of **$42.9M positive** for the first time at scale. Yes, GAAP looks ugly because of acquisition accounting and $36.9M in one-time charges. But strip those out and you have a business crossing into profitability on an operating basis while still growing top line at hyperspeed.

At $6.62, you're paying less than 2x sales for a company that dominates NFL, EPL, and now Liga MX data rights. The 52-week low was $3.83 — we're 73% above that. RSI at 39 is approaching oversold territory without any negative company news to justify the selloff. This is pure technical selling in a risk-off tape (beta 1.88).

The bear will point to debt. Fair — $821M is a lot. But it financed a transformative acquisition that expands GENI's platform from data provider to full-stack betting technology. Interest expense is painful now, but if normalized EBITDA holds at $40M+ quarterly, debt service becomes manageable within 18 months.

**My case:** GENI at $6.62 is a mispriced growth compounder in a temporary technical correction. Support at $6.39 (Bollinger lower) should hold. A bounce to $6.95–$7.22 (200/50 SMA) is the near-term base case, with $8.00+ achievable on any positive earnings revision. The risk/reward from here favors the bull.

# Bear Researcher

## Round 1

I appreciate the enthusiasm, but let's be honest about what's happening: this stock is in a confirmed downtrend with accelerating negative momentum, and the fundamentals underneath are far more fragile than the bull narrative suggests.

Yes, revenue grew 65% — but **net loss widened to -$76.7M** in Q2, free cash flow burned **-$105.7M**, and the balance sheet just took on **$790M in new debt** overnight. Tangible book value went from +$214M to **-$846M**. That's not "acquisition accounting" — that's a leveraged bet that may not pay off.

The bull says normalized EBITDA is positive at $42.9M. Great — but interest expense alone was $13.8M in one quarter. Annualize that and you're looking at ~$55M in interest against ~$170M normalized EBITDA before taxes, capex, and integration costs. The math doesn't scream "cheap at 6x forward P/E" when forward EPS of $1.07 assumes flawless execution post-acquisition.

Technically, this is worse than "approaching oversold." MACD histogram is **-0.14 and expanding** — that's accelerating downside, not a dip to buy. Price is below the 10 EMA, 50 SMA, and 200 SMA. Volume is rising on down days (5.9M shares on Sep 15 vs 3.3M average). That's distribution, not accumulation.

The Liga MX deal? Nice press release, but it won't move the needle on $821M of debt or stop the bleed. Options traders are positioning defensively (Zacks flagged unusual activity), and there are **zero insider purchases** at these levels. If management believed in the story, they'd be buying.

Support at $6.39? The February crash took this stock to $5.79 in a single week. With beta 1.88 in a risk-off environment, $6.00 is very much in play. The 52-week low of $3.83 is the tail risk.

**My case:** GENI is a leveraged, cash-burning acquisition story trading on hope. The technical trend is down, momentum is negative, and the balance sheet is the weakest it's ever been. Holders from $8+ are underwater and likely to capitulate on any further weakness. Protect capital now.
